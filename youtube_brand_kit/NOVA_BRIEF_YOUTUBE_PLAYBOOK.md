## 10. Content strategy

Use four repeatable content pillars:

| Pillar | Share | Viewer outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| AI news explained | 40% | Understand an important update quickly |
| Tech opportunities | 30% | Discover a program and take action before the deadline |
| AI tools and workflows | 20% | Learn one useful tool or workflow |
| Building Nova Brief | 10% | Follow the project journey and build trust in the founder |

Recommended starting schedule: **five Shorts per week** for eight weeks.

- Monday: major AI news
- Tuesday: opportunity alert
- Wednesday: useful AI tool
- Thursday: AI news or myth explained
- Friday: building Nova Brief or weekly roundup

Start by testing **6:00–9:00 PM Pakistan time**. After 28 days, schedule around the “When your viewers are on YouTube” report rather than keeping a guessed time.

Add one 5–8 minute source-based explainer every two weeks. Shorts create reach; useful long-form explainers create deeper trust and qualified watch hours.

## 11. The Nova Brief Shorts formula

Aim for 35–55 seconds for most news and opportunity Shorts. Use longer Shorts only when the story genuinely needs the time.

- **0–2 seconds — Hook:** state the surprising change, benefit, deadline, or risk
- **2–8 seconds — What happened:** one plain-language sentence
- **8–25 seconds — Essential context:** only the facts needed to understand it
- **25–42 seconds — Why it matters:** connect the story to students, builders, or tech careers
- **42–52 seconds — Action:** what the viewer should try, verify, or apply for
- **Final 2–3 seconds — CTA:** ask one relevant question or point to the full source/site

Visual rules:

- Use 1080×1920 vertical video
- Keep faces and key text away from the right-side interface and bottom caption area
- Change the visual pattern every 1–3 seconds
- Use large on-screen captions with 3–7 words per line
- Put the main promise on screen in the first second
- Do not begin with a logo animation; show the value immediately
- End in a way that loops naturally to the opening line when possible

## 12. Title system

Keep titles specific and honest. Lead with the result, change, deadline, or question.

Templates:

- `[COMPANY] Just Launched [OPPORTUNITY] — Who Can Apply?`
- `This New AI Tool Can [SPECIFIC RESULT]`
- `[AI COMPANY] Changed [FEATURE] — Here’s Why It Matters`
- `Do This Before Applying to [PROGRAM]`
- `I Built [NOVA BRIEF FEATURE] — Here’s What Broke`
- `[NUMBER] AI Tools Students Should Know in 2026`
- `The Truth About [POPULAR AI CLAIM] in 45 Seconds`

Avoid vague titles such as “Big AI News Today,” false urgency, all-caps sentences, and unrelated celebrity/company names.

## 14. Calls to action

Use one CTA per video, chosen to match the content:

- “Would you use this? Tell me why.”
- “Comment ‘LINK’ if you want the official source pinned.”
- “Save this before the deadline.”
- “Which tool should I test next?”
- “Get the daily brief at NovaBrief.tech.”
- “Subscribe for the next update without the hype.”

Do not ask for likes, comments, subscriptions, and website clicks all in the same Short.

## 15. News and AI trust standard

- Prefer the original company announcement, official program page, research paper, or government source
- Put important sources in the description and pin the most useful source
- State “reported,” “announced,” “confirmed,” or “rumored” accurately
- Show the verification date for deadlines and program requirements
- Correct errors publicly and quickly
- Use licensed or self-created visuals; a credit line does not automatically grant permission
- Add original explanation, testing, commentary, or reporting to every video
- Do not mass-produce near-identical AI voice-over slideshows
- Disclose realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered depictions when required

## 16. Weekly analytics review

Review results every seven days, but make strategic decisions after at least 10 comparable Shorts.

Track:

- viewed versus swiped away
- audience retention and the first major drop
- average view duration and average percentage viewed
- rewatches or loops
- shares and comments per 1,000 views
- subscribers gained per 1,000 views
- website clicks and newsletter sign-ups
- returning viewers

Decision rules:

- If viewers leave in the first seconds, rewrite the opening; do not blame the topic immediately
- If retention is good but reach is weak, test a clearer first frame and title
- If views are high but subscriptions are low, sharpen the channel promise and CTA
- If one topic produces both retention and subscribers, turn it into a named series
- Recreate the winning structure, not the exact same video

## 17. 30-day setup and growth checklist

### Days 1–3

- Apply the name, handle, description, links, profile image, banner, and watermark
- Turn on the Home tab and build the four core playlists
- Configure upload defaults, permissions, and account security
- Audit old Shorts: keep relevant videos, update weak titles/descriptions, and unlist only content that seriously conflicts with the new brand

### Days 4–7

- Publish the channel trailer
- Batch-produce the first five Shorts
- Create a consistent caption, source-card, and end-frame system
- Add the website CTA and source links to every relevant upload

### Weeks 2–4

- Publish five Shorts per week
- Reply to meaningful comments within the first 24 hours
- Post one poll or useful community update each week if the Posts tab is available
- Turn the strongest Short into a deeper 5–8 minute explainer
- Review analytics each Sunday and change only one major variable at a time

## 18. Monetization path

Treat YouTube first as a distribution engine for the Nova Brief newsletter and website, not only as ad inventory.

Revenue ladder:

1. Newsletter and website audience growth
2. Relevant sponsorships with clear disclosure
3. Affiliate links for tools actually tested
4. Premium opportunity alerts or research product, if the product supports it
5. YouTube Partner Program when eligible

Do not buy views, subscribers, comments, or watch time. Do not publish generic AI-generated batches; YouTube evaluates originality and whether content is mass-produced or repetitive.

## 19. Final pre-publish checklist for every Short

- The first frame communicates a clear benefit
- The story is accurate as of today
- Primary source checked and linked
- Names, numbers, dates, and deadlines verified
- Original commentary or demonstration included
- Captions corrected
- Music and footage rights confirmed
- AI disclosure selected correctly
- Title is specific and honest
- One relevant CTA included
- Related video added when available
- Comments checked after publishing
